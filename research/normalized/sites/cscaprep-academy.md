About the CSCA

About

The exam is the door. We help you walk through it.

The China Scholastic Competency Assessment is the standardised test international undergraduates sit to apply to Chinese universities. We provide independent practice questions, explanations and a free study community.

What the CSCA actually is

A short, honest read on the exam — what it tests, what it doesn't, and where it sits in your application.

Maths required

Mathematics is required for every applicant. Physics and Chemistry depend on the program; Professional Chinese is for Chinese-taught programs only. Maths, Physics and Chemistry are each 48 multiple-choice questions in 60 minutes.

University requirements

Check each receiving university’s current notice. CSCA results do not automatically replace school credentials, language evidence or another required test.

Scholarship requirements

Scholarships have their own eligibility and selection rules. A CSCA score can be part of an undergraduate application, but it does not guarantee funding.

Who we are

An independent practice project maintained by its contributors. We do not claim official examiner affiliation or guarantee admission.

CSCA Prep Academy provides practice tools and worked explanations. The project contributors maintain this material; it is not an official exam paper or an admissions service.

Our editorial policy is to distinguish official requirements from preparation advice. We link to primary sources for exam rules and direct applicants to the exact university and scholarship notice for their program and intake. We do not publish score-to-admission predictions or claim unverified reviewer credentials.

If you find an error, contact the team with the page or question identifier, your reasoning and any supporting official source. Corrections update the relevant content date. Individual study help depends on community availability.

What we're not

Not an agency — we don't submit applications on your behalf.

Not affiliated with CSC, MOE, or any Chinese university.

Not a placement service — we don't guarantee admissions or funding.

We're an independent study platform. The CSCA itself is administered by the China Scholarship Council; the syllabi, dates, and policies are set by CSC, not us.

Common questions

Do I need to take the CSCA to apply for a Chinese scholarship?
Check the current notice for the exact degree, teaching language and scholarship. CSCA can be required for undergraduate applications, including English-taught programs. Do not assume a waiver or that a school qualification substitutes for the test.
What is the difference between the CSC scholarship and a university scholarship?
Chinese Government Scholarships and university-funded awards have different funding sources and selection rules. Compare the coverage, separate application steps and restrictions on holding multiple awards in the actual notices.
Can I apply for a Chinese scholarship without a Chinese language background?
Some programs are taught in English, but language evidence and any funded preparatory study depend on the scheme. Check the university’s exact language requirements and the scholarship’s coverage before applying.
How competitive are Chinese scholarships?
Competition depends on the scheme and applicant pool. We do not have a representative dataset that supports a universal competitive CSCA score or funding probability. Use published eligibility and selection criteria for the specific award.
When should I start preparing my scholarship application?
Start early enough to gather documents, complete any required tests and meet each published deadline. University, embassy nomination and scholarship deadlines can differ; keep a separate calendar for each.

Prepare for the CSCA by checking your target program’s current subject requirements, choosing the correct paper language, and planning around the official registration and result deadlines. This page is an independent preparation checklist for the 2027 intake.

Subjects and format

Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry are 48 multiple-choice questions in 60 minutes per subject. Professional Chinese has 80 questions in 90 minutes. Each subject is reported on a 100-point scale. Requirements depend on the receiving university and degree; do not assume all applicants sit every subject.

Check the syllabus hub and the current official syllabus. The English or Chinese paper choice for fundamental subjects should follow your program’s instructions. Professional Chinese requirements, categories and exemptions must also be checked against the admission notice.

Dates and registration

Use the exam calendar for recorded sitting dates and current registration status. Register through csca.cn. Registration and scholarship applications are separate processes; a scholarship nomination does not book an exam.

For any later sitting whose registration dates have not been announced, wait for the official notice rather than assuming a monthly window. Confirm the local date and time on your candidate instructions.

MBBS and other program requirements

For medicine, engineering and other degrees, read the exact program’s required subject combination and teaching-language rules. Requirements should not be inferred from a broad degree label such as MBBS. Record the notice URL, intake year and result deadline with your notes.

Passing scores and scholarships

There is no universal score that guarantees admission or funding. We do not publish national percentile estimates or automatic conversions between practice percentages and official results. See scoring and requirements for a practical checklist.

A preparation checklist

Confirm programs, subjects and paper languages.

Read the current syllabus and assess your baseline.

Practise topics and review explanations.

Use fresh timed mocks to assess pacing.

Complete official technical checks before exam day.

Check results and submit them as instructed by each institution.

The complete preparation guide offers study-plan templates. Adapt them to your baseline; a fixed number of weeks is not a score guarantee.

Register for the CSCA through csca.cn. Confirm your receiving university’s required subjects and paper language, then follow the current sitting notice and portal instructions. Exam registration is separate from a university application or scholarship nomination.

Before creating a booking

Read the target program’s current admission notice. Record required subjects, paper language and the deadline for submitting results. Use the exam calendar to plan, then confirm the actual sitting and registration status on the official portal.

If a registration window has closed, check a later sitting. Do not assume late registration, a recurring monthly opening date or a waiver of your university’s deadline.

Registration checklist

Create or sign in to your own official account.

Choose an available sitting and confirm exam mode and local time.

Select the subjects and paper languages your program requires.

Provide identification details and a photo according to the portal’s current specifications.

Check the fee, payment method and booking summary before paying.

Save the booking confirmation and follow the admission-ticket and technical-check instructions.

Exact document formats, photo dimensions and payment methods can change. Use the requirements displayed in the official booking flow instead of an unofficial checklist.

Fees

The published structure is RMB 450 for one subject or RMB 700 for two or more. Confirm the current amount on csca.cn before paying. Do not pay a study group to register on your behalf or share your account password with a coordinator.

Subjects and paper language

Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry can be offered in English or Chinese. Professional Chinese is in Chinese, with categories and exemptions set out in official guidance. Check the receiving university’s exact subject requirements before choosing.

After booking

Check your identity details, subjects and exam mode. Convert the official Beijing-time schedule for the place you will sit. Complete the required technical checks and use the current candidate instructions to prepare your equipment and room.

If something is wrong, use official support channels promptly. Keep university and scholarship deadlines separate from the exam booking. See the exam-day checklist and results guide.

Interpret the result

A mark is feedback on that practice set. For example, 36 correct answers out of 48 is 75% correct. This is not an official score conversion, an estimate of national rank or a prediction of your real exam result.

A high practice mark may reflect familiar questions. Use new sets where available, and check whether you can explain the method without seeing the solution. Read scoring and requirements before using a practice result to plan an application.

Original worked example

For x² − 5x + 6 = 0, factor to obtain (x − 2)(x − 3) = 0. The roots are 2 and 3. Substituting them into the original equation checks the answer. This example illustrates solution review; it is not an official CSCA question.

Your student attempt history requires your account. Do not assume a review link is public, and do not share login details. When asking for help, share the question and your reasoning while keeping personal account information private.

CSCA subjects are reported on a 100-point scale. There is no single score that guarantees admission or a scholarship. Check the receiving university’s requirements for your exact program and intake; do not use a practice percentage as an admissions forecast.

How to read a score requirement

Start with the current university admission notice. Record the required subjects, paper language, score-report deadline and any stated subject minimum. A scholarship can have a separate application and selection process. If a notice does not publish a minimum, ask the international admissions office rather than assuming a cutoff.

Check

Program and intake

Degree, teaching language and entry year

Required subjects

The exact subject combination in the notice

Minimum scores

Only a minimum explicitly stated for that program

Report deadline

When the university must receive the result

Funding

Scholarship criteria, application route and separate deadline

Keep the notice URL and publication date with your notes. A threshold from a different year or degree is not necessarily applicable.

Practice scores are learning feedback

The platform calculates marks from the questions in a practice exam. For example, 36 correct answers out of 48 is 75% correct. That arithmetic describes this practice set; it is not an official score conversion, national percentile or prediction of admission.

We do not publish national score distributions or score-to-scholarship probability tables. A representative dataset and a disclosed method would be needed to support such claims. Compare your mistakes across topics and fresh timed sets instead of repeatedly memorising one mock.

Setting a useful study target

List the subjects your target programs require.

Note any published minimum, keeping its exact source.

Practise under the time limit shown for your chosen mock.

Review both wrong answers and correct guesses.

Re-test with a fresh set and check whether the reasoning improves.

If a requirement is unclear, ask the university. A platform score cannot resolve an unpublished admissions rule.

Scholarship decisions

CSCA may be part of an undergraduate application, but funding decisions also depend on the scholarship’s own requirements and application file. Language-teacher scholarships, provincial awards and university awards are separate schemes. Do not assume that a particular CSCA band qualifies an applicant for all of them.

If your result is below a published minimum

Contact the receiving university about its rules and deadlines. Another sitting may be useful if its result can arrive in time, but improvement and admission are not guaranteed. See results and retake planning.

CSCA 12-Week Study Plan: Topics, Practice & Mocks

Plan 12 weeks of CSCA preparation with topic review, timed practice, mistake analysis and mock exams, adapted to your required subjects.

This is a 12-week study plan for a student organising preparation around the subjects required by their target program. The plan assumes 2-3 hours of study per day, 5-6 days a week. Adjust the volume if you have less time per day (push the schedule out to 14-16 weeks) or more (compress to 8-10 weeks).

The plan is structured as four phases:

Diagnostic and foundation (weeks 1-3)

Topic drilling (weeks 4-7)

Mock exams and reflection (weeks 8-10)

Final review and exam-week prep (weeks 11-12)

Each week has a recommended mix of: practice problems, content review, mock exams, and rest.

Phase 1: Diagnostic and foundation (weeks 1-3)

Week 1 — Diagnostic

The week is a single task: take a full diagnostic exam to find your baseline.

Day 1-2: Sign up on the platform if you haven't. Take the placement diagnostic in all three STEM subjects. Don't study first. The point is to find your starting point.

Day 3-4: Read the diagnostic report. Review the topics and explanations in your practice results; the platform does not estimate an official percentile.

Day 5: Set your target score. Use only minimums published for your exact program and intake; a practice score does not predict admission.

Day 6-7: Rest. Process the diagnostic.

By the end of week 1, you should have a target score and a per-topic map of your weak areas.

Week 2 — Foundation: the topics you are weak in

The week is about closing the gaps the diagnostic exposed.

Daily: 2-3 hours. 60% on your weakest subject, 40% on the other two.

Format: Read the relevant subject guide section. Then do 15-20 practice problems from the topic. Mark every wrong answer for review.

End of week: Re-do the topic problems without looking at the answers. The goal is to internalise the patterns, not to memorise the specific problems.

If your diagnostic was uniformly weak (no topic above 60%), spend this week on Mathematics only. Mathematics is the foundation for the other two STEM subjects, and the topic structure is the most uniform across all three.

Week 3 — Foundation: rhythm and pacing

The week is about getting used to the test format.

Daily: 2-3 hours. Half on content, half on timed practice.

Timed practice: One 48-question mini-mock per day. The platform's per-topic mini-mocks are ideal. Get used to the 60-minute section format.

End of week: Take a second diagnostic (the same one as week 1). Compare to the week-1 result. If you've improved by 5+ points per subject, you're on track. If you haven't, revisit the week-2 plan.

Phase 2: Topic drilling (weeks 4-7)

Week 4-5 — Subject 1 (your strongest)

Spend two weeks on your strongest subject, getting it to 80+.

Daily: 2-3 hours. 100% on this subject.

Format: One full topic per day (e.g. "Functions" or "Mechanics"). Read the syllabus guide, work through 20-30 platform problems on that topic, review the explanations for every wrong answer.

End of week 5: Take the subject mock exam. Target 80+. If you're below, repeat the week on the topics where you missed the most points.

Week 6-7 — Subject 2 (your middle)

Same pattern as weeks 4-5, for your second-strongest subject. Target 75+ on the mock at the end of week 7.

If you only have time for two subjects, take the subjects required by your target program. Most engineering / science programs require Math + Physics + Chemistry, so all three.

Phase 3: Mock exams and reflection (weeks 8-10)

Week 8-9 — Full mock cycle

The week is three full mock exams (one per STEM subject), with reflection in between.

Day 1: Mathematics mock. Timed. 60 minutes. Mark yourself honestly.

Day 2: Review every wrong answer. Read the explanation. Re-do the question without looking at the answer. Identify the topic cluster of your wrong answers.

Day 3: Physics mock. Same format.

Day 4: Review Physics.

Day 5: Chemistry mock.

Day 6: Review Chemistry.

Day 7: Rest, or one more mock if you are behind.

By the end of week 8, you should have three mock scores, all within 5 points of your target. If any is more than 10 points below target, repeat weeks 4-5 on that subject.

Week 10 — Targeted review

The week is to fix the topics that the mocks flagged.

Daily: 2-3 hours. 100% on the topics where the mocks identified gaps.

Format: 30 problems per topic, all from the platform's question bank. Aim for 80%+ on the topic before moving on.

End of week: Take a second full-mock cycle (same as weeks 8-9). The improvement from week 8 to week 10 should be at least 3 points per subject.

Phase 4: Final review and exam-week prep (weeks 11-12)

Week 11 — Consolidation

The week is to lock in what you know, not to learn new things.

Daily: 2-3 hours. 70% review, 30% new practice.

Format: A short review of each subject's notes (your own, or the platform's per-topic summaries). Then one 15-question topic-set per day, rotating subjects.

End of week: One more full mock. This is your last chance to identify gaps. Anything you miss at this point is a bet on the exam — adjust your target accordingly.

Week 12 — Exam-week prep

The week of the exam. Almost no new content.

Mon-Wed: One last review of the topic summaries. Practice the topics you have been weakest on. Don't push for new material.

Thu: Light review only. Confirm your seat assignment letter, ID, packing list, and travel plan.

Fri: Travel to the venue if it's not local. Do not study.

Sat (or exam day): The exam.

Sun: Rest. You earned it.

The single biggest mistake students make in week 12 is to cram. Last-minute cramming has been shown to lower scores by an average of 5%. If you are not ready by Friday, you are not ready. The exam is what it is; the score is what it is.

Daily study rhythm

Across all 12 weeks, the daily rhythm that works for most students:

2-3 hours of focused study (no phone, no Slack, no Discord)

60-90 minutes of one subject per session, then switch

10 minutes of rest between subjects

5-10 minutes of physical movement between sessions (walk, stretch)

A real meal before the next session

The platform's per-subject diagnostic and per-topic problems are designed for this rhythm. One topic per session, 15-30 problems, full review of the wrong answers.

If you are not on track

If at any point your mock score is more than 10 points below target:

Don't skip weeks. Re-do the current week on the weak topics.

Don't push the schedule out unless the gap is more than 15 points. Adding weeks doesn't help if the same patterns keep producing the same mistakes.

Do get help. The platform's practice problems surface the same patterns. If you keep missing the same topic, the issue is the topic understanding, not the practice volume. Read the subject guide for that topic from start to finish.

If at any point you are ahead of schedule:

Don't coast. Use the extra time to drill the topics you are weakest in.

Don't skip the mocks. Mocks are the only realistic way to find the gaps that practice alone won't expose.

What this plan does not include

This plan is for the academic preparation. It does not cover:

The registration flow (see the how to register page)

The exam-day logistics (see the exam day checklist page)

The scholarship application flow (see the China scholarships page)

The Chinese language preparation (most programs don't require it; if yours does, add a separate Chinese study block to your weekly plan)

A 12-week plan focused on the three STEM subjects is the right starting point for most students. The platform's per-subject guides and mock exams are designed to fit this plan.

Use the current syllabus published on csca.cn as the authority for what to study. Our subject guides organise preparation topics and examples; they are not official syllabus documents or an assurance that every listed extension topic is examined.

Choose subjects before choosing a study plan

Mathematics is a fundamental requirement. Physics and Chemistry requirements depend on the receiving program. Professional Chinese is associated with Chinese-taught programs, with categories and exemptions specified in official guidance. Confirm the exact combination and paper language in your target university’s current notice.

Do not assume a universal combination for MBBS, engineering or another broad degree label. Record each program’s requirements separately and resolve differences with the admissions office before registering.

Mathematics preparation

Use the Mathematics guide to organise practice in sets and inequalities, functions, geometry and algebra, and probability and statistics. Match individual learning outcomes to the current official syllabus before allocating revision time.

Physics preparation

Use the Physics guide for topic practice in mechanics, thermodynamics, electromagnetism, optics and waves, and modern physics. These are the platform’s study categories. Confirm exact scope, terminology and required depth against the official syllabus; do not infer coverage from a category name alone.

Chemistry preparation

Use the Chemistry guide to practise concepts, chemical theory, properties and reactions, and experiments. Build a checklist from the official syllabus, then match practice questions to it.

Professional Chinese

Professional Chinese is a separate Chinese-language paper with 80 questions in 90 minutes. It has Humanities and STEM categories in official guidance. Check the category, requirement and any exemption for your degree. This platform currently focuses on Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry practice.

Format and candidate rules

Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry contain 48 multiple-choice questions in 60 minutes per subject. Follow the current candidate instructions for permitted equipment, reference materials and calculator rules. Do not infer those rules from the fact that questions are multiple-choice.

Open the official portal and obtain the syllabus for your subject and paper language.

Record the version or publication date when available.

List learning outcomes you can explain and those needing revision.

Work through a fresh practice set for each weak area.

Recheck the official instructions before the sitting.

The China Scholastic Competency Assessment (CSCA) is an assessment for international undergraduate applicants to Chinese universities, launched by the China Scholarship Council. Your receiving university’s current admission notice determines the subjects and reports you need. CSCA Prep Academy is an independent practice platform, not an exam organiser.

Each subject is scored on a 100-point scale. Mathematics is a fundamental requirement; Physics and Chemistry depend on the program. Professional Chinese concerns Chinese-taught degrees, with categories and exemptions set out in official guidance. Check the university notice rather than assuming a subject combination.

Registration and exam mode

Register through csca.cn. The portal lists sitting notices, booking steps, available modes and candidate requirements. Home-based, computer-based and paper-based options are described in official guidance, but availability must be checked for your actual sitting and location.

Use the exam calendar for recorded dates and registration status. Confirm the current candidate instructions and the local start time before the exam.

Fees

The published structure is RMB 450 for one subject or RMB 700 for two or more. Confirm the current amount and payment instructions on the official portal before paying.

How universities use results

CSCA results can be part of an undergraduate admission and scholarship application alongside school records, language evidence and other documents. They do not automatically replace a school qualification or guarantee a funding offer. See score requirements and scholarship routes.

The exam itself

The what, the how, and the when — answered plainly.

What is the CSCA?

The CSCA (China Scholastic Competency Assessment) is an assessment for international undergraduate applicants, launched by the China Scholarship Council. Check your target university’s current admission notice for required subjects, school credentials and language evidence.

How is the exam structured?

Mathematics is required for every applicant. Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry are each 48 multiple-choice questions in 60 minutes. Professional Chinese is 80 multiple-choice questions in 90 minutes and is only required for Chinese-taught programs. Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry can be sat in English or Chinese; Professional Chinese is in Chinese only.

What subjects do I need to take?

It depends on the program you are applying to. Mathematics is required for every applicant. Physics and Chemistry are required by program; Professional Chinese is only required for Chinese-taught programs. Check the admission requirements for your target program before registering — most universities publish a subject-by-subject list.

How is the exam scored?

Each subject section is scored out of 100 points. There is no negative marking. There is no national CSCA pass mark — each university sets its own admission cutoffs and scholarship requirements.

Can I retake the exam?

Check the official portal for another available sitting and ask your university which reports it accepts. Confirm the result deadline and whether subject scores from different sittings may be used together before paying.

What is on the official syllabus?

Use the current syllabus on csca.cn as the authority for your subjects. Our guides organise preparation topics and examples; match them to the official learning outcomes before choosing a study plan.

The practice platform

What you get with a sign-up, and how it works.

Is the practice platform really free?

Yes. Every question in the bank, every mock exam, the spaced-repetition review, and the daily challenge are free with a sign-up. We do not gate features behind a paywall.

What if I get stuck on a question or topic?

Message the team directly on WhatsApp for 1:1 help, or join the public cohort group for peer Q&A and study-plan sharing. There is no paywall on the help channel — it is open the moment you reach for it.

How many questions are in the bank?

Question availability changes as the bank is maintained. Use the subject and topic filters to see available practice, and check the exam list for current mocks.

How does the spaced repetition work?

Every question you answer creates a card. After each attempt you rate it as easy or hard. The platform uses an SM-2 schedule to bring the card back the next day, the next week, or in a month — tuned for a single sitting exam, not a vocabulary list.

Do I need a Chinese phone number or VPN?

No. The platform is built for international students, accessible from anywhere. Sign-up needs an email address. The exam itself is mainly taken online at home with a live proctor; offline centres are being added gradually in some countries.

What languages is the platform available in?

English, with full support for Chinese characters in questions and explanations where the original syllabus uses them. We do not yet support Spanish, French, or Arabic interfaces; that is on the roadmap.

Scholarships & the help channel

Where the CSCA score actually pays off, and how to reach the team for the rest.

Which scholarships does the CSCA unlock?

CSCA can be part of an undergraduate application, but each scholarship sets its own requirements. Check the current CSC, university, provincial or language-teacher scholarship notice. No CSCA score guarantees funding, and some schemes have separate language-test or nomination requirements.

How do I get help with the rest of the application — the personal statement, the professor email, the document review?

The platform is the practice tool — questions, mocks, spaced repetition. For the rest of the journey, message the team on WhatsApp or join the public cohort group; templates, study plans, and feedback are shared openly there.

Mathematics

Syllabus · Mathematics

Mathematics — the CSCA syllabus, topic by topic.

Forty-eight questions in sixty minutes. The CSCA Mathematics section is the most content-heavy of the three — four modules, each pulling roughly a quarter of the marks. The good news: the syllabus is short, the patterns are repeatable, and the traps are predictable once you know them.

The syllabus, topic by topic

4 modules. Four of them carry the bulk of the marks; the rest round out the picture. Read in order — each topic assumes the previous one.

01

Sets and Inequalities

What to study

Set notation and operations (union, intersection, complement), Venn diagrams, the empty set, subsets, and the cardinality axioms. Linear, quadratic, and absolute-value inequalities; interval notation; solution sets on the real line. Systems of inequalities and the graphical method for finding the feasible region.

What the exam tests

Set-builder notation in problem statements, three-set Venn diagram problems, and the boundaries of quadratic inequalities (including the sign of the leading coefficient). The CSCA likes asking for the cardinality of a complement or a difference.

Common traps

Confusing the solution of x² < 4 (|x| < 2) with x < 2 (half-open interval, not symmetric).

Forgetting that |x| < 4 has an interior, not a single point, and writing the answer as a single value.

Mixing up union and intersection when a Venn diagram has three overlapping sets.

Popular programsEngineeringComputer ScienceEconomics

02

Functions

What to study

Definition, domain, codomain, range, and graph reading. Linear, quadratic, polynomial, rational, exponential, and logarithmic functions — their properties, transformations, and inverses. Composition, piecewise functions, and the behaviour of f(x) as x → ±∞. Trigonometric functions as a separate block: identities, graphs, and the unit circle.

What the exam tests

Identifying a function from its graph, reading the domain and range, and applying transformations. The exponential / logarithm block is the second-largest within Functions; expect at least one problem that requires using ln or log to linearise a relationship.

Common traps

Confusing the graph of y = log(x) (defined only for x > 0) with y = √x (also defined only for x ≥ 0) — different domains.

Thinking f(g(x)) = g(f(x)) — composition is not commutative.

Forgetting that sin²x + cos²x = 1 is a Pythagorean identity, not an angle-addition identity.

Popular programsMathematicsStatisticsData Science

03

Geometry and Algebra

What to study

Coordinate geometry (lines, circles, conic sections, distance and midpoint formulas), vectors (operations, dot product, projection, angle between vectors), and matrices (operations, determinants, inverse, solving linear systems). Analytic geometry of the plane; basic three-dimensional geometry. The sequence-and-series block sits here: arithmetic and geometric progressions, sums, and limits.

What the exam tests

Vector problems almost always involve the dot product or angle computation; matrix problems almost always ask for the inverse or the determinant. The conic section is usually a single problem — circle or ellipse, identifying the centre and the radius / semi-axes.

Common traps

Sign errors in the 2×2 determinant (the ad − bc order, not ad + bc).

Forgetting that a parallel projection of a vector onto another vector requires the unit vector in the direction of the target.

Confusing arithmetic (constant difference) with geometric (constant ratio) progressions — the sum formulas are different.

04

Probability and Statistics

What to study

Counting (permutations, combinations, the multiplication principle). Classical probability, conditional probability, Bayes’ theorem, and independence. Random variables (discrete and continuous), expected value, variance, and the binomial / normal distributions. Descriptive statistics: mean, median, mode, variance, standard deviation, and reading a box plot.

What the exam tests

Bayes’ theorem shows up at least once per paper, almost always in a two-stage conditional form. The binomial coefficient C(n, k) and the standard normal table are the two tools the CSCA expects you to have at hand without prompting.

Common traps

Reversing the conditional: P(A|B) is not P(B|A). Bayes’ theorem swaps them, with a multiplication by P(B) / P(B).

For binomial, the variance is np(1−p), not np — easy to forget the (1−p) term.

Treating events as independent when they are actually mutually exclusive (or vice versa).

A few clarifications

A note on trigonometry

Trigonometric functions live inside the Functions module in the CSCA syllabus, not in a separate "Trigonometry" block. You will see sin, cos, and tan on graphs, in identities, and in the geometry questions; you will not see a pure trig identities drill.

Calculus is not on the syllabus

Limits and derivatives appear only in the context of the sequence-and-series block (limits of sequences) and the function behaviour (as x → ±∞). Do not spend time on differentiation or integration as standalone topics — they will not appear.

How to use

A routine that works on a real schedule.

Six steps, one daily habit, and the platform handles the rest. The plan below is what most successful candidates do — adapt it to your own pace, but don't skip the daily challenge.

The six-week routine

Follow this from your first day of prep to your exam-day mock. Each step builds on the one before.

01

Map your baseline

Day 1 — half a sitting

Sign up, take the placement diagnostic (10 questions per subject, mixed difficulty). The platform scores you against the official topic distribution and gives you a per-topic weakness map. This is what you'll work from.

02

Pick a study schedule

Day 2 — 15 minutes

Choose a target exam date in the platform. The scheduler generates a six-week, four-week, or two-week plan based on how much time you have. Each plan mixes topic drill, mixed practice, and review — with built-in buffer for the days life happens.

03

Run the daily challenge

20 minutes a day

Five questions per day — randomised across subjects and topics, weighted by your weak areas. The challenge is the single biggest predictor of score improvement; streaks compound.

04

Review what you miss

10 minutes after every session

Every question you get wrong (or guess on) gets re-scheduled via SM-2 spaced repetition. Read the explanation, rate the question, move on. The platform surfaces the same trap again in 1, 3, or 7 days until it sticks.

05

Take weekly mocks

60 minutes every Saturday

A 48-question mock per subject, exactly the real-exam format, timed. The mock report tells you which topics cost you marks, where you ran out of time, and which questions you rushed.

06

Use the last 10 days for revision

Final sprint

Stop new content. Re-do every mock. Re-do every card the spaced-repetition system flagged as forgotten. The platform's "final-week plan" does this for you; you just press Start.

The principles behind it

Why the routine works. Read these once; refer back when motivation dips.

Practise in the right format

Multiple-choice, single-correct-answer, English-or-Chinese. Every practice question on the platform matches the real CSCA format. Don't waste time on free-response or essay-style practice — that's a different exam.

Mark every guess honestly

A guess you got right is a wrong answer you don't understand. The platform's spaced-repetition engine treats honest self-rating as the only signal that matters.

Read the wrong answers, not just the right one

CSCA wrong answers are designed to catch specific misconceptions. The explanations name the misconception and the underlying principle — that's the content the next question will test.

Mock exams > endless practice

A 60-minute mock, scored and reviewed, teaches you more than 60 minutes of free practice. The format, the pacing, the stamina — you don't build any of those in topic drill.

Track per-topic, not per-subject

"I scored 78 in Physics" tells you nothing. "I scored 78 in Physics because I lose 6 marks to Modern Physics and 4 to Optics" tells you what to study next week. The platform surfaces this breakdown automatically.

Sleep on it

Spaced repetition is built on the science of memory consolidation. A question you answered tonight will come back tomorrow, then in 3 days, then in 7. Trust the schedule; don't cram the same question three times in one night.
