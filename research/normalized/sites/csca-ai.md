Your first seven days of CSCA preparation

Start with your university’s requirements and the official syllabus, then use a short practice set to find your first gaps. This is an example routine you can adjust to your subjects and exam date.

Day 1

Confirm your papers

Read each target university’s admission notice. Write down the CSCA subjects, test language and application deadline it requires.

Day 2

Mark the official syllabus

Open the organizer’s PDF for every required subject. Mark each topic as familiar, partly known or new; this becomes your study checklist.

Day 3

Take a short baseline

Try the free five-question practice set in each available subject without notes. Record the questions you guessed or missed. A short set is a starting signal, not a full readiness score.

Day 4

Repair one gap

Choose one missed syllabus topic. Read a lesson or worked example, then solve a few similar questions and explain the method in your own words.

Day 5

Review your errors

Redo the questions you missed without looking at the answer. Note whether each error came from a concept gap, a calculation slip, a misread term or time pressure.

Day 6

Practise the next topic

Study another weak topic from your official syllabus and answer a fresh short set. Compare the result with your first attempt.

Day 7

Choose the longer plan

Use your topic checklist and error log to choose a 4-, 8- or 12-week plan below. Schedule topic study, review and full-length mocks where available before your exam date.

This is CSCA.ai study guidance, not an official timetable. The Shanghai government’s CSCA preparation guide recommends checking university subject requirements, using the official syllabus, practising weak areas and keeping an error log. Try a free short practice set to begin.

Keep a CSCA practice error log

After each practice set or mock, record the syllabus topic, your answer, the correct method, why you missed it, and when you will try a similar question again. Review the log each week to choose which topics to study next.

For example, in our own Mathematics practice question about a set with four elements, choosing 16 instead of 15 proper subsets means you counted the whole set. Write “subtract one from 2ⁿ” as the correction, then solve a new subset question without notes the next day. This is a study example, not an official exam question.

What Is the CSCA Exam? Subjects, Format & Dates

The CSCA (China Scholastic Competency Assessment) is an entrance exam used by participating Chinese universities for international undergraduate applicants. It covers Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry and Professional Chinese, depending on the program. Official registration, dates and fees are published on csca.cn.

What is CSCA?

The China Scholastic Competency Assessment (CSCA) is an undergraduate entrance exam used by participating Chinese universities to evaluate international applicants.

The Chinese embassy's CSCA notice says the China Scholarship Council organizes the exam with Chinese university experts. Universities use the results in admission and scholarship reviews and set their own subject requirements. CSCA.ai provides independent preparation; official registration is at csca.cn.

Quick answers

What is the CSCA exam?

The China Scholastic Competency Assessment (CSCA) is an undergraduate entrance exam that participating Chinese universities use to evaluate international applicants. It is an academic readiness check that sits alongside the rest of your application, not a standalone admission decision.

Is the CSCA exam the same as HSK?

No. HSK measures Chinese language proficiency. CSCA assesses academic readiness for undergraduate study through Mathematics and, depending on the program, Physics, Chemistry and Professional Chinese. Check your target university’s admission requirements for each test.
Compare CSCA, HSK and Gaokao

Which CSCA subjects do I need to take?

Mathematics is required for all applicants; Physics and Chemistry depend on the program. Chinese-taught applicants generally take Professional Chinese. The CSCA exam introduction also lists it for Chinese Government Scholarship applicants, but Shanghai government guidance exempts fully English-taught applicants. If you are applying for a scholarship to an English-taught program, confirm the exact subjects with your university or scholarship office before registering.

What is the CSCA exam format?

The Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry papers each have 48 single-choice questions to answer in 60 minutes, in Chinese or English. The Professional Chinese paper has 80 questions in 90 minutes. Every paper is scored from 0 to 100.

Can I take the CSCA exam in English?

Yes. Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry are offered in English or Chinese. Professional Chinese is taken in Chinese. Choose the language your university requires, then confirm the option shown for your exam session before registering.
Check the official CSCA language options

How do I take the CSCA exam?

CSCA offers home-based online and test-centre exams. The options available to you depend on your country and sitting, so follow the mode shown on your booking and admission ticket. For a home exam, read the current official candidate guide and complete its device, room and system checks before test day.
Prepare with the home-based exam checklist

Who has to take the CSCA?

International applicants to bachelor's programs at participating Chinese universities may need the CSCA. For the 2027/28 Chinese Government Scholarship, the Chinese Embassy in South Sudan says undergraduate applicants must take it, while applicants to other degree programs do not. Check your university and scholarship route for the subjects required.
Read the Chinese Embassy's 2027/28 scholarship FAQ

Do master's applicants need the CSCA exam?

The CSCA is designed for international undergraduate applicants. The Chinese Embassy in South Sudan's 2027/28 Chinese Government Scholarship FAQ says applicants to other degree programs do not need CSCA for that scholarship route. If you are applying for a master's degree, check your university's own admission requirements rather than registering for an undergraduate exam by assumption.
Read the embassy's scholarship FAQ

When is the CSCA exam held?

The published calendar lists six sittings from November 2026 through June 2027: November and December 2026, then January, March, April and June 2027. Each sitting has its own registration window. Check csca.cn for exact dates and any changes before booking.

How much does the CSCA cost?

Published notices give the fee as 450 CNY for one subject and 700 CNY for two or more subjects. Confirm the current fee on csca.cn when you register.
Read the CSCA exam fees and registration guide

Is the CSCA exam free?

No. The official CSCA exam charges 450 CNY for one subject or 700 CNY for two or more subjects, according to the exam organiser's published fee table. CSCA.ai's five-question Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry practice sets are free, but they are independent preparation, not official exam registration. Check the current fee on csca.cn before paying.
Check the official CSCA fee table

What is the CSCA passing score?

There is no single national pass mark. Each subject is scored from 0 to 100, and each university decides what scores it accepts for each program.

What is the CSCA exam pass rate?

The official CSCA exam introduction does not publish one overall pass rate. It says each paper is scored from 0 to 100 and universities set their own admission standards. Ask your target university what score it expects for your program instead of relying on an unsupported pass-rate percentage.
Read the official CSCA scoring explanation

Is the CSCA exam hard?

The content is familiar to most science-stream students, but each Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry paper gives you about 75 seconds per question, so speed and accuracy are the real challenge. Timed practice under exam conditions helps most.
Compare CSCA with the SAT, ACT, A-Level and IB

Can I use a calculator in the CSCA exam?

The official 2025 Mathematics syllabus does not say whether calculators are allowed. Check the candidate notice and permitted-materials instructions for your session on csca.cn before test day. Practising calculations by hand is still useful preparation.

When do CSCA results come out?

The current official CSCA exam introduction says results are released within 10 working days after the exam. Check your session notice and leave extra time before university application deadlines. Each subject is scored separately from 0 to 100.

How long is a CSCA score report valid?

The Chinese Embassy in South Sudan's 3 November 2026 FAQ says CSCA score reports are valid for two years from the exam date and that a valid report can be used for the 2027/28 Chinese Government Scholarship. Check your university and scholarship route for their own score-submission rules before relying on an earlier result.
Read the embassy's score-validity answer

Can I retake the CSCA?

Yes. Because the CSCA runs several sessions a year, you can register for a later session and sit a subject again. Leave enough time for the new result to arrive before your universities' deadlines.

Is this CSCA the same as the IMA CSCA certificate?

No. The China Scholastic Competency Assessment is an entrance exam for Chinese universities. The IMA's Certified in Strategy and Competitive Analysis (also CSCA) is an unrelated professional accounting certificate.

Is CSCA.ai the official CSCA exam provider?

No. CSCA.ai is an independent preparation platform. Official rules, dates and fees are set by the exam organisers and published on the official CSCA website, csca.cn, so confirm them there for your session.

The CSCA exam format is simple: every question is single-choice. Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry each have 48 questions in 60 minutes, offered in Chinese or English. Professional Chinese has 80 questions in 90 minutes, in Chinese only. Each subject is scored from 0 to 100, and there is no single official pass mark.

Exam: China Scholastic Competency Assessment (CSCA), the admission test for international applicants to undergraduate programs at Chinese universities

Subjects: Professional Chinese (Humanities or STEM track), Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry

Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry: 48 single-choice questions, 60 minutes each, in Chinese or English

Professional Chinese: 80 single-choice questions, 90 minutes, Chinese only

Scoring: 0–100 in each subject, reported separately

Maximum score: 100 per subject; there is no official combined total

Pass mark: none set nationally; each university sets its own requirement

Calculator: Check the candidate instructions for your sitting; prepare to work without one

Results: the current official exam introduction says scores are released within 10 working days after the exam; check your sitting notice.

One quick note on the name: CSCA is also an accounting certificate from the IMA (Certified in Strategy and Competitive Analysis). This guide is only about the China Scholastic Competency Assessment, the test for students applying to bachelor's programs in China.

What is the CSCA exam format?

The CSCA has four subjects, but you sit only the papers required for your application route and target programs. Mathematics is required for every applicant; Physics and Chemistry depend on your major and university. The official CSCA exam introduction lists Professional Chinese for Chinese Government Scholarship undergraduate applicants, while the Shanghai government CSCA guide says applicants to fully English-taught undergraduate programs are exempt. These published instructions differ. If you are applying to a fully English-taught scholarship program, confirm the exact requirement with your target university or scholarship office before booking. For other applicants, Professional Chinese is required for Chinese-taught programs. Choose the Humanities or STEM track your university specifies. For dates, fees and registration, see our CSCA exam 2026–27 guide.

Mathematics: 48 single-choice questions, 60 minutes, Chinese or English, scored 0–100. Required for all applicants.

Physics: 48 single-choice questions, 60 minutes, Chinese or English, scored 0–100. Required by many science, engineering and medical programs.

Chemistry: 48 single-choice questions, 60 minutes, Chinese or English, scored 0–100. Required by many science, medical and agriculture programs.

Professional Chinese (Humanities or STEM): 80 single-choice questions, 90 minutes, Chinese only, scored 0–100. Required for Chinese-taught programs. The official exam introduction also lists it for Chinese Government Scholarship applicants, but Shanghai government guidance exempts fully English-taught applicants. Confirm your route and track with your university or scholarship office.

Every question is single-choice: you pick the one correct option from the choices given. There are no essays, no written solutions and no partial credit for working shown on paper. This makes the CSCA a test of two things at once: whether you know the high school content, and whether you can apply it quickly and accurately under time pressure.

Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry are offered in Chinese or English. Choose a paper language your target universities accept, then use the language in which you can answer most accurately under time pressure. The official CSCA exam introduction says to follow each university’s application requirements when selecting a language. Professional Chinese is always taken in Chinese. How long is the CSCA exam?

Each science subject lasts 60 minutes and Professional Chinese lasts 90 minutes. That works out to about 75 seconds per question in Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry, and about 67 seconds per question in Professional Chinese. Your total CSCA exam time depends on how many subjects you take: Mathematics alone is one hour, while Professional Chinese, Mathematics and Physics add up to three and a half hours of testing.

The official CSCA calendar now lists two-day sittings from 14–15 November 2026 through 26–27 June 2027. The November registration window closed on 21 October 2026; later sittings have separate windows. Start times and paper arrangements should be checked in the current organiser notice and your admission ticket, in Beijing Time (UTC+8). Convert the confirmed time to your local zone before test day. How is the CSCA scored?

Each subject is scored on a scale from 0 to 100, and each subject is reported separately. Your score comes from the single-choice questions you answer correctly. Because 48 questions do not divide evenly into 100 points, some weighting or conversion must be used, but the official notices we checked do not publish the exact formula. Be careful with websites that claim to turn your raw answers into an exact CSCA score.

The official information we reviewed does not mention negative marking for wrong answers. If the candidate rules on csca.cn confirm this when you register, the sensible strategy is to answer every question, even if you have to make an educated guess in the final minute. Remove the options you know are wrong first, then choose.

What is the CSCA max score?

The CSCA max score is 100 in each subject. There is no official combined total. Some students add their subject scores together, for example 300 for three subjects, but that is an informal number. A university can look at each subject on its own, so a strong Mathematics score may not make up for a weak Physics score if your program cares about both. Ask your target university how it reads results from several subjects.

Is there a CSCA minimum score or pass rate?

No single official pass mark exists for the CSCA. It is not a pass or fail exam. Instead, each university sets its own requirements, and these can differ by program and by scholarship. Some university notices describe CSCA scores as a minimum admission criterion, while others describe them as one reference in an overall academic review.

The same goes for the CSCA exam pass rate: no official pass rate has been published, and without a national pass mark the idea does not really apply. If a website quotes a pass rate or a fixed cut-off score without linking to a university or official source, treat it as a guess. The reliable way to find your minimum score is your target university's admission guide, its admissions office, or the scholarship notice you are applying under.

Is CSCA Mandatory? Who Needs the CSCA Exam in 2026–27

Is CSCA mandatory? Yes for Chinese Government Scholarship bachelor applicants and participating universities in 2026–27, with a wider rollout planned by 2028.

Is CSCA mandatory? For 2026 and 2027 entry, yes if you apply for an undergraduate degree with the Chinese Government Scholarship, or to a university that has adopted the CSCA. Master's and PhD applicants do not take it. The official rollout plan expects the requirement to cover all international undergraduate applicants by 2028.

What it is: the China Scholastic Competency Assessment, a standardized test for international applicants to bachelor's programs at Chinese universities. It is not the IMA accounting certificate that shares the CSCA name.

Mandatory for 2026–27 entry: undergraduate applicants to the Chinese Government Scholarship, and applicants to universities that have made CSCA part of their admission rules.

Not required: master's and PhD applicants.

Planned: the official rollout plan expects the requirement to extend to all international undergraduate applicants by 2028.

Mathematics: required for every applicant. 48 single-choice questions, 60 minutes, in English or Chinese.

Physics and Chemistry: required depending on your major. 48 questions and 60 minutes each.

Professional Chinese: 80 questions, 90 minutes, Chinese only. Required for Chinese-taught programs and listed for Chinese Government Scholarship undergraduates; confirm any exemption with your university.

Sessions: CSCA normally runs in January, March, April, June, November and December. Check csca.cn for exact dates.

Is CSCA mandatory for every student going to China?

No, not yet. The CSCA is being introduced in stages. Right now it is mandatory for a defined group of applicants and not yet required for others. Whether you need it depends on three things: the level you are applying for, the university you choose, and how you are paying for your studies. Your nationality on its own does not decide it. For a one-page overview of subjects, format, fees and the reported session dates, see our CSCA exam 2026–27 guide.

If the test is new to you, start with our plain-English guide to what the CSCA exam covers. In short, it measures whether you are academically ready for a Chinese bachelor's program. Each subject is scored from 0 to 100, and you can sit it at an authorized test center (computer-based or paper-based) or at home with live online proctoring.

Who needs to take the CSCA in 2026 and 2027?

The Shanghai government's official CSCA FAQ states that the test is mandatory for all international students applying to undergraduate programs at Chinese Government Scholarship universities for the 2026 and 2027 academic years. In practice, three groups of students need a CSCA score report now:

Chinese Government Scholarship (CSC) bachelor applicants, whether you apply through the Chinese embassy in your country (Type A) or directly to a university (Type B).

Self-funded applicants to universities that have written CSCA into their own international undergraduate admission rules.

Applicants for provincial, city or university scholarships at universities that ask every bachelor applicant for a CSCA score report.

Some universities have gone beyond the minimum. Beijing Forestry University, for example, has said it will require the CSCA score report from all international undergraduate applicants, including self-funded students and applicants to every scholarship program. Other universities still admit self-funded students without it for now. This is why you must read the current international admission guide of each university on your list, not just a general article.

Which universities require CSCA?

There is no single, permanent list that answers this for every student, because more universities join each year and each one publishes its own rules. Check in this order: the participating university information on the official CSCA website, then the international admissions page of each university you like, then the Chinese embassy or consulate in your country if you apply through the Type A scholarship route. If you are still choosing where to apply, you can compare Chinese universities and scholarships on WiseAdmit, the company behind CSCA.ai.

Universities that have published CSCA instructions or subject requirements for international bachelor applicants include, for example, Beijing Language and Culture University, Beijing Forestry University, Sichuan University, Nanjing University of Information Science and Technology, China University of Mining and Technology, Guangdong University of Foreign Studies and Hebei University of Technology. This is not a complete list. When a university says it requires CSCA, its page usually also lists the subjects each major needs, and that subject list matters as much as the yes-or-no answer.

Humanities, business, law or social sciences, English-taught: Mathematics, plus any other papers required by the university or scholarship.

Humanities, business, law or social sciences, Chinese-taught: Mathematics, the university’s required Professional Chinese track, and any required science paper.

Science, engineering, agriculture or medicine, English-taught: Mathematics, plus the science and language papers required for the program and scholarship.

Science, engineering, agriculture or medicine, Chinese-taught: Mathematics, the required Professional Chinese track, and Physics and/or Chemistry where required.

Some universities ask for both Physics and Chemistry for certain majors, so always confirm the exact subjects for your program.

Do English-taught programs need CSCA?

English-taught programs can still require CSCA. Take the papers listed by your university. The organizer lists Professional Chinese for Chinese Government Scholarship undergraduates, while some university notices waive it for English-taught programs. Confirm your program’s subjects and any scholarship exemption before booking.

Who is exempt from CSCA?

Master's and PhD applicants: CSCA is designed for undergraduate admission, and postgraduate applicants are not required to take it.

English-taught bachelor applicants: confirm any Professional Chinese exemption with the university; take its other required CSCA papers.

Chinese Language bachelor applicants: may be exempt from Professional Chinese with a valid HSK Level 4 score, according to the Shanghai official FAQ.

Self-funded applicants to universities that have not adopted CSCA yet: not required for 2026–27 entry, but check again every admission year.

Notice what is not on this list. We have not seen any official rule that lets an SAT, A-level, IB or national school-leaving result replace CSCA at a university that requires it. Those results can still strengthen your application, but they are not a substitute for the score report. If a university tells you that you are exempt, ask for it in writing and keep that email with your application documents. What changes with CSCA in 2028?

The official rollout plan expects CSCA to apply to all international undergraduate applicants to Chinese universities by 2028, whether they hold a scholarship or pay their own fees. Universities and education sources also report that formal minimum admission standards will be announced. Treat 2028 as the planned direction rather than a final, detailed rule: the Shanghai official FAQ, for example, only confirms the 2026 and 2027 academic years.

What this means for you is simple. If you plan to start a bachelor's degree in China in September 2028 or later, assume you will need CSCA wherever you apply. If you start earlier, you may still need it, depending on the university. No national minimum or cut-off score has been published yet, so any passing score you see online is unofficial. CSCA sample-question searches often mix three different things: official samples from the exam authority, independently written practice questions, and files that somebody calls a ‘past paper’ without showing where they came from. Treat those differently. Use the official syllabus to decide what to study; use original practice questions to discover gaps; use a timed mock to rehearse the pace. None of these can guarantee your real score. This guide shows a practical routine you can adapt even if you have only a week and no paid question bank.

Know what the official exam describes

The official exam introduction lists Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry as separate 60-minute subjects with 48 objective single-choice questions each, available in Chinese or English. Professional Chinese has a different 90-minute, 80-question format. Your application route and target university determine which subjects and language you need. A practice pack for the wrong subject or language is an inefficient use of time.

Start with the actual sample-question PDFs on the official CSCA site: Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry. Checked on 25 October 2026, these are official examples, not complete previous-year papers or a full live test; the files carry notices restricting republication. For an independently written mock, look for its author, subject, language, date, answer explanations and coverage of the current syllabus. Do not assume an unverified PDF is a real past paper. The official CSCA exam introduction gives the format; your target university’s admission notice is the final check for its own subject rules.

Use a four-step practice cycle instead of endless full mocks

A repeatable practice cycle

01

Map the syllabus

List each official topic and mark your confidence as strong, developing or unfamiliar. Choose practice questions that test the unfamiliar areas first.

02

Do a diagnostic set

Work without notes and keep time. Record wrong answers by topic and error type: knowledge gap, calculation, misread wording or time pressure.

03

Repair the weakest topics

Relearn the underlying idea, solve a few untimed examples and explain why the correct option is right and the distractors are wrong.

04

Take a full timed mock

Use 60 minutes for a 48-question Mathematics, Physics or Chemistry rehearsal. Review afterward; the review is the part that changes your next week's plan.

Make an error log after every set. Use five columns: topic, what you chose, why the correct answer works, error type, and the next action. For example: ‘Maths — quadratic equations — chose B — forgot that the discriminant must be non-negative — knowledge gap — review the rule and solve three new questions tomorrow.’ If you understood the rule but rushed the calculation, mark it ‘calculation’ instead. If you misread ‘not’ or a unit, mark it ‘reading’. If you ran out of time, mark it ‘pacing’. This distinction tells you whether to study the concept, practise the method, slow down, or rehearse under a clock. Reattempt the missed concept with a new question after a gap; repeating the same answer from memory can create false confidence. A study by Roediger and Karpicke found that retrieving information through tests improved delayed retention compared with repeated study (Psychological Science, 2006; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16507066/). That research supports self-testing as a learning method; it does not predict a CSCA score. Record the date, subject, time used and number correct, but compare scores cautiously when question difficulty differs.

A seven-day practice example

Day 1: Confirm the subjects and language your target university requires. Read the official syllabus and take a short diagnostic without notes. The free five-question practice set shows the interface and a few gaps; it cannot estimate a full-exam score.

Day 2: Choose your two weakest topics. Study one short explanation or worked example for each, solve fresh questions untimed, and write down what each wrong answer taught you.

Day 3: Try new questions on those topics before opening your notes. Check the explanation after you commit to an answer. Recalling the method is more useful than recognising it on a page.

Day 4: Mix old and new topics in one set. Use a modest time limit. Record whether each error came from a concept, calculation, wording or speed.

Day 5: Repair the most common error type. If algebraic manipulation caused several mistakes, practise that step on new problems before taking another full test.

Day 6: If you have a reliable mock aligned with the official syllabus, take one complete 48-question Mathematics, Physics or Chemistry paper in 60 minutes. That is about 75 seconds per question on average; mark a hard question, move on, and return if time allows.

Day 7: Review every wrong or guessed answer, update your topic plan, and revisit missed ideas with different questions. Rest before starting the next cycle.

Check any mock’s source, date, subject, language, explanations and coverage of the official syllabus. A useful mock helps you find weaknesses and rehearse timing; its score is not an official prediction. CSCA Home-Based Exam Checklist: Devices, Room and Practice

A CSCA home-based exam checklist covering devices, room, passport, official system checks and when to choose a test centre instead.

Three common setup questions

For a home-based CSCA sitting, prepare both your subject knowledge and the equipment needed to enter a supervised exam. The official home-exam notice describes a primary computer and a secondary phone or tablet, with remote invigilation. It says the content, duration and question type match the centralized computer exam. Technical specifications and software instructions can change between sittings; read the current candidate guide, admission ticket and official exam dates before you practise with the setup below. Reviewed 25 October 2026.

CSCA currently lists home-based online and onsite computer-based tests. Check the formats available for your country and sitting on csca.cn.

One week before: review the official instructions

Read the current home-based exam notice, operating guide and any video supplied for your sitting.

Check the exact required operating system, browser or exam client, camera, microphone, internet and secondary-device arrangement in that guide. Do not assume a device that worked for another online test will work here.

Run any official equipment or environment check when the portal offers it. Resolve failures while there is still time to contact official support.

Prepare the original ordinary passport used for registration and ensure the identity photo and account details match.

Write down start time in the time zone named in the official notice and set a reminder for the earlier check-in time.

The two-device setup is part of supervision, not an optional study aid. The primary computer is used for the exam, while a phone or tablet acts as the secondary position according to the official instructions. Arrange both so the required view is clear and power is available; follow the exact positioning diagram in your candidate guide.

The day before: set up a compliant room

Choose a quiet space where you can work without visitors or interruptions. Clear the desk of notes, extra devices and anything the current exam rules prohibit. Check lighting so your face and identity document can be verified. Tell household members when the test will be running, but do not let anyone enter the monitored space during the exam. If the official guide asks for a room scan, understand how to complete it before test time.

A short rehearsal

01

Test the setup

Use the official system check with the actual devices, power supply and network you plan to use.

02

Rehearse the handoff

Place the passport nearby for check-in, then follow the invigilator's rules about where it may remain during the test.

03

Practice to the clock

For Mathematics, Physics or Chemistry, rehearse 48 single-choice questions in 60 minutes, then review accuracy and pacing.

04

Plan a help route

Locate the official support contact and what to do if a technical problem occurs before or during the sitting.

During the exam: follow the proctoring rules

Stay within the monitored view and follow the invigilator's instructions. CSCA's official user agreement describes restrictions on leaving the camera view, using unauthorized materials or communication devices, and obstructing monitoring equipment. If you have a legitimate interruption or a device problem, report it through the exam's prescribed channel instead of trying to fix it in a way that might appear to break a rule.

If the official system check fails

Use CSCA's own equipment or environment test to verify exam access; a CSCA.ai mock only checks your subject practice. If the official check fails, write down the exact message and time. Retry with the same primary computer, secondary phone or tablet, power supply and network you plan to use on exam day. Review the current candidate guide's camera, microphone, software and device-position instructions, then run its test again. If the problem remains, contact the official support route in your sitting's notice early enough to receive help. A device that worked for another online exam is not proof it meets CSCA's current rules.

During a live sitting, tell the invigilator about a technical fault through the prescribed channel. Do not leave the monitored view, change the exam device, close monitoring software or restart the exam without instructions. The CSCA platform user agreement identifies unauthorized device changes and obstruction of monitoring as exam-rule issues. Follow the invigilator and your sitting's current candidate instructions if an interruption occurs.

Three common setup questions

These answers apply to a home-based sitting. Use your own session's candidate guide and admission ticket for the final rules.

Can I take it on a phone alone? No. The official home-exam notice describes a primary computer and a secondary phone or tablet for invigilation. Check the candidate guide for supported software and devices.

Can I use this checklist for a test-centre exam? No. Follow your centre’s check-in, identity and equipment instructions instead. The academic paper format may match, but the room and device workflow differs. Check your admission ticket for the confirmed format and location.

What should stay on the desk? Keep the original registered passport ready for identity checks, then follow the invigilator's directions. Remove notes, extra devices and anything the current rules do not allow. Do not assume personal scratch paper or a calculator is permitted.

Sources and review date (25 October 2026): CSCA home-exam notice, platform user agreement, exam introduction, Shanghai government CSCA guide and Chinese embassy FAQ. These describe exam modes, location guidance, two-device supervision and conduct rules. Your sitting’s current notice, booking, admission ticket and invigilator instructions control the exact format, setup and check-in procedure.
