II. What is the CSCA? Understanding the CSCA Exam

The China Scholastic Competency Assessment (CSCA) is an entrance test for international
undergraduates, created to give Chinese universities a common benchmark for judging whether
applicants are ready for degree study.

The test is developed and administered under the China Scholarship Council (CSC). Beginning
with the 2026 intake, the CSCA score report (transcript) becomes a mandatory document for
undergraduate applications to Chinese Government Scholarship universities.

Before CSCA: For a long time, getting into Chinese universities as an international student was
confusing and inconsistent. The main challenge was that students came from different countries
with different education systems and curriculums. This made it extremely difficult for Chinese
universities to fairly compare applicants' academic abilities, especially in subjects like mathematics
where curriculum rigor and grading standards varied dramatically between countries. A student
with 90% in math from one country might have a completely different skill level than a student
with 90% from another country. There was no standardized way to assess whether international
students had the foundational knowledge needed for Chinese university programs.

What CSCA Does: The CSCA completely changes this. It creates one clear, standardized test that
all universities recognize and trust. For international students, this is a game-changer: you finally
know exactly what you need to prepare for, how you'll be evaluated, and what schools are looking
for.

What Is It? According to the China Scholarship Council (CSC), the CSCA is a fair and scientific test
that measures whether you're ready for university-level study. Think of it as China's way of making
international admissions as structured and rigorous as the gaokao (the national college entrance
exam for Chinese students) but designed to work for students from any educational background
around the world.

                                                                                      3

Students must take the CSCA exam before applying for CSC scholarships or universities that
require these results for admission.

Key Facts:

   ● First Global Exam: December 21, 2025
   ● 2026 Schedule: 5 sessions annually (January, March, April, June, December)
   ● By 2028: Expected to be mandatory for ALL undergraduate applicants
   ● Registration Deadline: November 25, 2025 (for the December 21, 2025 exam session).
       Check the other deadline for other exam session on the official website (csca.cn)
   ● Official Website: https://csca.cn

Why the CSCA Matters:

The CSCA replaces the old document-based admission system with a transparent, standardized
evaluation comparable to China's gaokao (for domestic students) or SAT/A-Levels internationally.
It ensures:

   ● Fair evaluation across diverse educational systems
   ● Unified standards for academic readiness
   ● Merit-based selection for scholarships and admissions

V. Detailed Syllabus (Official 2025 Edition by CSCA)
Mathematics (100 Points)

   ● Sets & Inequalities: Set operations; Quadratic and rational inequalities.
   ● Functions: Domain/Range, Monotonicity, Parity; Power, Exponential, Logarithmic, and
       Trigonometric functions; Arithmetic/Geometric sequences; Basics of Derivatives/Calculus.
   ● Geometry & Algebra: Analytic geometry (Lines, Circles, Ellipses, Hyperbolas, Parabolas);
       Vectors; Complex Numbers; Solid Geometry (Space coordinates).
   ● Probability & Statistics: Classical probability; Mean/Variance; Normal distribution.

Physics (100 Points)

   ● Mechanics: Velocity/Acceleration; Newton’s Laws; Momentum/Impulse; Work & Energy;
       Circular Motion; Simple Harmonic Motion.
   ● Electromagnetism: Coulomb’s Law; Ohm’s Law; Magnetic Induction; Lorentz Force;
       Faraday’s & Lenz’s Law.
   ● Thermodynamics: Molecular theory; Ideal gas equation; First law of thermodynamics.
   ● Optics: Reflection/Refraction; Interference/Diffraction.
   ● Modern Physics: Photoelectric effect; Atomic structure; Nuclear physics.

Chemistry (100 Points)

   ● Basic Concepts: State changes; Chemical equations; Solution pH/Concentration; Ideal Gas
       Law.
   ● Properties & Reactions: Inorganic substances (Acids, Bases, Salts, Metals); Organic
       compounds (Hydrocarbons); Redox reactions; Ion reactions.
   ● Theory: Periodic trends; Chemical bonds/Intermolecular forces; Reaction
       rates/Equilibrium; Electrochemistry.
   ● Experiments: Lab safety; Gas identification; Purification methods.

Professional Chinese (100 Points)

This is NOT HSK. It tests academic language ability.

    ● Science Track (理科中文): Math symbols, Physics laws, Lab operations, Data chart
        interpretation.
    ● Humanities Track (文科中文): Literature, Philosophy, Law, History, Education, Politics.
    ● Question Types: Character recognition, Vocab fill-in-the-blank, Paragraph completion,
        Reading comprehension.

VI. Understanding China's Education System

How China's System Differs

1. Academic Rigor & Standards

   ● Gaokao legacy: The domestic entrance exam is known for its difficulty
   ● Foundation focus: Strong emphasis on fundamental concepts
   ● Problem-solving speed: Quick recall and application under time pressure
   ● Theoretical depth: Deep understanding over breadth

2. Teaching Style

   ● Lecture-based: More teacher-centered than Western universities
   ● Examination-oriented: Regular tests and assessments
   ● Group study culture: Students often study in groups
   ● Homework load: Expect substantial daily/weekly assignments

3. Academic Calendar

   ● Fall Semester: September - January
   ● Spring Semester: February/March - July
   ● Long summer break: July - August
   ● Short winter break: January - February

4. Language Environment

   ● Chinese-taught programs: Expect 100% instruction in Chinese
   ● English-taught programs: May still require basic Chinese for daily life
   ● HSK requirement: Most Chinese-taught programs require HSK 4-5
   ● Academic Chinese: Different from conversational Chinese (focus on specialized
       vocabulary)

IX. How to register for the CSCA
Where to register
All candidates register on the official CSCA website at csca.cn. This is the only place to create your
account, pick subjects, choose a test window, and pay the fee.

When to register
For the inaugural sitting, registration opens in November 2025. From 2026 onward, registration
windows are published ahead of each sitting across the year. Always check the portal for the latest
dates.

Step by step registration process

   1. Create your account on csca.cn with your full name exactly as in your passport. Verify your
       email and complete your personal profile.
   2. Fill in your application basics. Choose the program language and intended major direction
       so the correct exam subject options appear.
   3. Choose your subjects. Mathematics is required for all applicants, while Physics and
       Chemistry are optional (depend on the major). Professional Chinese (Humanities Chinese
       or STEM Chinese) is required for applicants applying for Chinese-taught programs.
   4. Pick a test window. The first global inaugural test will be held on December 21, 2025.
       From 2026 there are five sittings each year (scheduled in January, March, April, June, and
       December). Pick a schedule that will leave enough time for score release before your
       earliest university deadline.
   5. Select the test mode. Applicants can choose to do an at-home online test. Some locations
       will add offline centers over time.
   6. Upload your photo and ID details. Follow the portal’s format and size rules. Double-check
       passport number, name, and date of birth.
   7. Pay the fee through the portal. The current fee is RMB 450 for one subject or RMB 700
       total for two or more subjects. Keep your payment confirmation.

X. Technical Requirements (Strictly Enforced)
The exam uses a "Home-Based Online" format with AI and human proctoring. You must meet
these requirements or you cannot take the test.

Primary Device (Computer)

   ● OS: Windows 10 / 11 OR Windows 7 SP1 (64-bit).
   ●   ❌ MacOS is NOT SUPPORTED. (Do not use Mac).
   ● Storage: At least 2GB free space (Avoid installing on C drive if possible).
   ● Screen: Resolution ≥ 1366×768.
   ● Webcam: Built-in or external USB (≥720p). Must face you directly.
   ● Audio: Microphone and Speakers required.      ❌ Wireless Headphones/Earbuds are
        PROHIBITED.

Drafting Tools (CRITICAL UPDATE)

Standard paper is PROHIBITED. You must use one of the following:

   1. A small Whiteboard with an erasable marker.
   2. A blank sheet of paper inside a Transparent Document Sleeve/Bag with an erasable
        marker.
   ● Rule: You must erase your notes in front of the camera at the end of the exam.

XI. The "Two-Device" Setup Guide
You are required to have a Secondary Monitoring Device (Phone or Tablet).

1. Positioning

   ● Place it on a tripod or stable stand.
   ● Angle: 45 degrees behind you (to your side).
   ● View: Camera must see You (Profile) + Your Hands + Your Computer Screen + The Desk.

   ● Airplane Mode: ON (This is critical just in case someone calls your phone).
   ● Wi-Fi: ON (Enable Wi-Fi after turning on Airplane mode).
   ● Do Not Disturb: ON.
   ● Power: Keep it plugged into a charger, or make sure the battery is sufficient for the entire

   4. Testing Environment:
          ○ Private, quiet room with closed door
          ○ Well-lit space (natural or artificial light)
          ○ Clear desk - NO books, notes, or unauthorized materials

Exam Day Timeline & Rules

Pre-Exam (30-60 Minutes Before)

   1. Clear the Room: No other people, no books, no electronics.
   2. Login: You must log in 30–60 mins early.
   3. Verification: Complete Facial Recognition and hold your Original Passport to the camera.
   4. Environment Scan: You will be asked to pan your camera 360° around the room.

During the Exam

   ●   No Leaving: You cannot leave your seat (unless approved for emergency).
   ●   Eye Contact: Keep eyes on the screen. Looking away frequently triggers AI flags.
   ●   Drafting: Use your whiteboard/transparent sleeve only.
   ●   Tech Issues: If internet drops, log back in immediately (answers are auto-saved). If the

Post-Exam

   1. Submission: Wait for the "Scroll submitted successfully" message.
   2. Erase Notes: Show your whiteboard/sleeve to the camera and ERASE IT before closing

XVI. Frequently Asked Questions

   1. Is CSCA mandatory for all international students?
       CSCA is required for admission to undergraduate programs at Chinese Government
       Scholarship universities.

   2. Is there an age limit?
       No upper limit. However, candidates under 14 must have a guardian's consent and
       assistance to register.

   3. What are the universities that require CSCA?
       All chinese government scholarship (CSC) universities require students to take CSCA
       exam. You can check here university admissions pages and 2026 prospectuses:

       - Tianjin University (2026 Admissions Guide)
       - Nankai University (2026 Admissions Guide)
       - Zhejiang University (2026 Admissions Guide)

   4. What are the universities that don’t require a CSCA exam?
       As of November 2025, these are the universities not yet requiring CSCA for 2026 intake: -
       Tsinghua University (but if you wish to apply for a CSC scholarship, you need to take the
       CSCA exam).

       - Beijing Normal-Hong Kong Baptist University (BNBU)
       - University of Nottingham Ningbo China (UNNC)
       - Xi’an Jiaotong-Liverpool University (XJTLU)
       - Sino-British College, USST
       - CUHK-Shenzhen

   5. What exam subject should I take?
       The subjects you must take for the CSCA are determined primarily by your intended major
       and the language of instruction. Mathematics is MANDATORY for all applicants, regardless
       of whether you are pursuing Engineering, Medicine, or Design.

       For STEM and Medicine majors, you must take an additional science subject. Furthermore,
       if you are applying to any Chinese-taught program, you must include one of the two

                                                                                 27

       Specialized Chinese tracks (either STEM Chinese or Humanities Chinese) to demonstrate
       your academic language proficiency.

   6. What is the language of the exam?
       The language options depend on the subject. You do not have to take all subjects in the
       same language. For the three Basic Disciplines (Mathematics, Physics, and Chemistry), you
       have a choice to take the exam in either English or Chinese. This is designed to allow
       international students to use their strongest academic language for technical subjects.

       However, the Professional Chinese subjects (Humanities and STEM Chinese) are offered in
       Chinese ONLY, as the entire purpose of these tests is to measure your readiness to handle
       university-level material in the Chinese language.

   7. If I don't apply to a university that offers Chinese government scholarships, can I still
       take this exam?
       Students are welcome and encourage all students planning to study in China to take the
       CSCA exams. An excellent CSCA score report can be a strong asset in your application to
       any Chinese university, comprehensively demonstrating your academic abilities.

   8. Can I use a Mac if I install Windows on it?
       The official rule states "MacOS is not supported." Using Boot Camp or Parallels is risky as
       the anti-cheat software may flag the virtualization as a security threat. It is strongly
       recommended to borrow a native Windows laptop.

   9. Can I retake the exam if I fail?
       Yes. Since there are multiple sessions (Jan, March, June, Dec), you can pay to retake it.
       However, be careful with application deadlines - a June retake might be too late for a
       scholarship application closing in March.

   10.Can I change my exam subject or language after registering?
       No. Once the Admission Ticket is generated, information cannot be changed. Verify your
       choices carefully before paying.

   11.How many times can I retake the exam?
       There is no limit on the number of times you can retake the CSCA. As long as you pay the
       exam fee again, you can register for the next available session. However, because of
       application deadlines and the exam schedule, you will realistically only have 1 or 2 chances
       to get a score that works for a specific September intake.

   12.Is there a chance for me to study in China if I fail the exam?
       Yes, you can retake the exam unlimited times, or apply to Chinese universities that don’t
       require CSCA for 2026 intake. Scores are valid for two years.

   13.Is paper-based testing available?
       Not for December 2025. The first global exam is exclusively Home-Based Online (or
       Computer-Based at select centers). Paper exams are currently suspended; check the
       website for future updates.
